01 · The brief, decoded

The logo has one job.

Make Outpost the first place a founder under 30 thinks of when they need help to grow — recognized globally, recalled instantly. T-Hub asked for the story first and the mark second. So that is the order of this deck.

ProductA digital T-Hub campus for founders anywhere, minus the co-working. Mobile-first website.→ Must read in a small mobile header first.
Three pillarsFounders, community, resources.→ Every part of the mark maps to one of the three.
Long-term ideaThe founder circle: get help, build, come back as a mentor.→ A loop is a ready-made story.
FeelClean, simple, young, soothing, instant recall, global.→ Few elements, calm palette, no clutter.
FormTypography-led, plus a standalone symbol for favicon and app icon.→ Wordmark and symbol designed as one.
T-Hub tieIts own identity, with the line “Built and backed by T-Hub”.→ An endorsed lockup; color that sits beside orange and grey.
AvoidMasthead serifs (Mint, ET)Rockets, lightbulbs, handshakes, gears, globesBusy gradientsCopies of the T-Hub markStock or AI-generated marksMilitary or remote “outposts”
01b · Who walks through the door

Four ages, one open door.

The brief caps the core audience at about 30, with mentors and partners right behind them. We designed for the 19-year-old and the 45-year-old at the same time — then looked for what they agree on.

The first idea17–21“Will anyone take me seriously?”Needs belonging and credibility, fast. The mark must be shareable — never childish.
The first company22–25“I'm doing this alone.”Needs proof that real help exists — and that it's free. Calm, warm, on their side.
The first scale26–30“Just show me the top three.”Needs speed: top matches only, no fluff, no forms. Sharp and efficient.
The return30+“Is this worth my name?”Judges credibility in seconds. Needs restraint — a mark that sits well beside global partners.
What they agree onPremium, to a 19-year-old, isn't price. It's being taken seriously.

03 · The Open Circle

Every founder who gets help comes back to give it.

01ArriveThrough the open door. One click with LinkedIn or Google — no forms until a feature needs them.
02Get helpMentors matched by sector, stage and need. Credits and perks from global partners.
03GrowGrants, corporate challenges and programs, surfaced as top matches. Momentum.
04Come backThe founder who was helped becomes the mentor someone books — and the next founder steps through the door.

Outpost is not a remote fort. It is the base that comes to you — T-Hub's campus, lit up wherever a founder is.

05 · The symbol

Three parts. Three pillars.

Nothing in the symbol is decoration. Take any part away and one of T-Hub's three pillars goes missing with it.

CommunityThe ringFounders, mentors and partners who have done it before. Drawn open, never closed — a circle you can enter.
ResourcesThe doorThe 45° opening is access: mentors, credits, grants behind one sign-in. It faces up and to the right — where every founder wants to go.
FoundersThe dotYou. The only warm thing in the mark, in T-Hub orange warmed one step. It sits in the doorway: arrived, not yet finished.

06 · The wordmark

Lowercase, like a first name.

Outpost speaks to founders as peers, so the name is set in lowercase and drawn as a single line. The first o carries the symbol; the long descender of the p becomes a ledge for T-Hub's endorsement.

One stroke16 units on a 100-unit x-height. Medium weight: confident at launch-banner size, still open at 72 px.
Round terminalsEvery end is round. Nothing sharp, nothing institutional — the same softness as T-Hub's own letters.
One circle, repeatedo, p and o share the symbol's circle (r 42). The u is a clean arch with no tail and the s is drawn wide with a diagonal spine, so the name reads in one even rhythm.
Drawn, not typedNo typeface is used. Every letter is a hand-built vector — original and trademarkable.
07 · The logo family

One mark, three ways in.

Pick by the space you have and whether T-Hub's backing needs to be said out loud. When in doubt, use the primary wordmark.

Primary wordmark

The default. Website header, product, social, merch. At small header sizes the endorsement moves to the footer and sign-in screen.

Endorsed lockup

Launch events, partner and corporate decks, press — anywhere T-Hub's backing should be stated. Use from 280 px wide.

Symbol

Favicon, app icon, social avatar, stickers — square or tiny spaces where the name is already known.

08 · Motion

The logo tells its story in 1.6 seconds.

Motion verbs from the noun list: arrive, orbit, return. Use the intro on launch screens, event stages and video end cards — never on every page load. Press play.

0.00 s
Arrive0.0 s · the dot drops in through the door
Orbit0.4 s · it travels the ring, picking up help
Climb0.9 s · past mentors, partners, grants
Settle1.4 s · back in the doorway, 4% overshoot
ReturnLaunch cut · a hollow dot joins the ring

Easing: cubic-bezier(.65, 0, .35, 1) for the orbit, back-out for the arrival. City cut: four dots light the ring in turn — Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad — then fade back to the single founder dot.

11 · Typography

Type with character.

Three families, each with one job. Golos Text carries the headlines — clear, contemporary and built for screens. Cactus Classical Serif carries the story: leads, quotes and the word a headline leans on. DM Sans does the everyday work in body text and the product. All three are open-source on Google Fonts, so mentors, partners and city teams can use them freely.

14 · Incorrect use

Eight ways to lose the story.

Each of these breaks one part of the meaning — the founder, the door or the circle. If a layout seems to need one of them, use a different variant instead.

Don't recolor the dot. The founder is always Signal.
Don't move the door. It faces up and to the right.
Don't close the circle or drop the dot.
Don't stretch, squash or skew.
Don't add gradients, shadows or glows.
Don't redraw it lighter or bolder.
Outpost
Don't retype it — and never as a masthead serif.
Don't place full color on Signal; the founder vanishes.
